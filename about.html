Counsellor · Clinic Lead · Co-founder

A career spent sitting with what is hard to say

Isabel Camilleri is a warranted counsellor in Malta. She has been in practice since 2009, working with young adults and adults, then increasingly with couples and families whose relationships have become a source of pain rather than rest.

She is a co-founder and Clinic Lead of Vita Mea Clinic in Mosta — a psychology and counselling practice built around a simple idea: mental health care should feel accessible, human, and tailored to the person in the room, not to a template.

Before co-founding the clinic, Isabel spent years as a counsellor in post-secondary education, including long service at MCAST, alongside private practice and corporate wellbeing work. Those settings taught her the same lesson in different languages: people want to be taken seriously, and they want a space that does not make them smaller for needing help.

Training and professional standing

Isabel holds a Master's in Counselling and a Bachelor of Psychology from the University of Malta. Her Master's research — Essential ingredients of couple therapy — explored what supports therapeutic work with distressed couples in Malta, including the therapist's own positioning and the factors that belong to the couple themselves.

She is a member of the Malta Association for the Counselling Profession and is listed on the Association's public register of warranted counsellors. She has also facilitated support-group work with Mental Health Association Malta.

You can read a fuller clinical summary on her Vita Mea practitioner profile, including specialisations and the populations she sees.

How Isabel thinks about the work

Isabel's therapeutic approach is holistic and integrative. She pays attention to emotional awareness, to the patterns that keep repeating between people, and to change that can survive the week after the session — not only the hour inside it.

She is particularly drawn to relationship and family dynamics: the public face of a couple that looks warm, and the private life that feels managed or small; the blended family that is trying to become a household; the parent who has lost the thread with a teenager. Anxiety and grief sit close to this work. So does the quieter question of personal growth — who you are when the roles fall away.

Clients often come after they have already tried to think their way out of the problem. Counselling, in Isabel's room, is less about being given answers and more about being accompanied while you find a truer one.

Why Vita Mea exists

Vita Mea — “my life” — is the clinic Isabel wanted people in Malta to be able to walk into. A place with warranted practitioners, evidence-informed approaches, and a reception that does not feel like a waiting room for something clinical and cold.

The clinic now offers individual, couples, family and specialist therapy, as well as work with children, adolescents and organisations. Isabel's own caseload stays close to the work she knows best: adults, couples, families, anxiety, grief, and workplace wellbeing.

If you would like to begin, enquire at Vita Mea Clinic and ask for Isabel. If another practitioner is a better match, the team will say so.
